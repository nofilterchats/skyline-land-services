Solar & Wind Leases: The Fine Print That Actually Matters

Solar and wind developers are calling more landowners every year, and the pitch is usually the same: steady passive income for doing very little. That part is often true. What's in the fine print determines whether it's a good deal or a decades-long regret.

The length of the lease is the real number

These leases commonly run 20 to 30 years, sometimes longer. Before you think about the payment, think about what else happens on that timeline — resale plans, family succession, other uses for the land. The lease term is arguably a bigger decision than the dollar figure.

Look for an escalation clause

Without one, your payment can stay flat for the entire term while land values and energy prices move around it. A rent-escalation clause tied to a clear schedule or index protects you from being locked into today's number for the next three decades.

Ask about decommissioning — specifically

Who removes the equipment when the lease ends, and who pays for restoring the land to its prior condition? This should be spelled out in the agreement, not handled with a verbal assurance.

A reluctant company is a signal

If a developer discourages you from getting the lease independently reviewed, or pushes urgency around a deadline that doesn't need to exist, treat that as information. A company confident in its offer has no reason to rush you.
